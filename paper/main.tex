\section{System Model}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{System Description}

We consider the multi-UAV mmWave ISAC network illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:system}. It consists of $K$ UAVs ($\mathcal K={1,2,\ldots,K}$ ), $M$  ground users ($\mathcal M$), $N$ static ground targets ($\mathcal N$), and a central controller (CC) which served by leader UAV.

The UAVs are equipped with $N_{t}$-element uniform linear arrays (ULAs) and fly at a fixed altitude band. Each UAV transmits a superposition of $M$ communication streams and one dedicated sensing stream, and it receives the target echoes of that transmission. Every ground user and target is considered static and equipped with a single antenna. The CC collects state information, runs the trajectory, power, and beamforming policy, and returns the resulting control to the UAVs. The mission horizon is divided into $T$ slots of duration $\Delta t$.

\subsection{A2G mmWave Channel Model}
\textbf{Probabilistic LoS model.} Let $\theta_{\rm el}$ denote the elevation angle (in degrees) of the UAV as seen from the ground node. The LoS probability follows the sigmoid model of Al-Hourani \emph{et al.}:
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm LoS}(\theta_{\rm el})=\frac{1}{1+a\exp\!\big(-b(\theta_{\rm el}-a)\big)}
\end{equation}
where $a,b$ are environment-dependent constants.

\textbf{Large-scale fading.} With $d_{k,m}=\max\{\|\mathbf p_k-\mathbf u_m\|,d_0\}$ and reference gain $\beta_0=(\lambda/4\pi d_0)^2$, the LoS/NLoS-weighted average path-loss gain is
\begin{equation}
\bar\beta_{k,m}=P_{\rm LoS}\,\beta_0\Big(\frac{d_0}{d_{k,m}}\Big)^{\alpha_{\rm L}} +\big(1-P_{\rm LoS}\big)\beta_0\Big(\frac{d_0}{d_{k,m}}\Big)^{\alpha_{\rm N}},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\rm L}<\alpha_{\rm N}$ are the LoS and NLoS path-loss exponents.
 
\textbf{Small-scale fading.} The channel from UAV $k$ to user $m$ follows a Rician model:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf h_{k,m}=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa\bar\beta_{k,m}}{\kappa+1}}\, \mathbf a(\theta_{k,m},\phi_{k,m}) +\sqrt{\frac{\bar\beta_{k,m}}{\kappa+1}}\,\mathbf g_{k,m},
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf g_{k,m}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\mathbf I_{N_t})$ and Rician $K$-factor $\kappa$. 

ULA steering vector
\begin{equation}
[\mathbf a(\theta,\phi)]_n=\exp\!\Big(j2\pi\frac{d_a}{\lambda}\,n\cos\phi\cos\theta\Big), \quad n=0,\dots,N_t-1.
\end{equation}

\textbf{notes for steering vector.} 
The steering vector is \emph{not} power-normalized, i.e., $\|\mathbf a\|^2=N_t$, so that $\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf h_{k,m}\|^2=\bar\beta_{k,m}N_t$. The array gain is thus embedded in the channel, and $\|\mathbf w\|^2$ equals the actual transmit power of a beam.
 
\textbf{Remark.} We adopt an ''expected-channel'' approximation: the LoS/NLoS states are averaged in the large-scale gain before the Rician component is applied. A strict two-state model yields a bimodal received-power distribution, and by Jensen's inequality the present model slightly overestimates SNR-concave metrics such as the ergodic rate. We validate the approximation against the two-state model via Monte Carlo simulation.

\textbf{Entities and their roles.} \emph{UAVs} are the only active transmitters. Each UAV is equipped with an $N_t$-element ULA aligned with the $+x$ axis and flies at altitude $z_k[t]\in[z_{\min},z_{\max}]$. In every slot it transmits a superposition of $M$ communication streams and one dedicated sensing stream, and it receives the target echoes of that transmission. \emph{Users} at $\mathbf u_m=(x_m,y_m,0)^T$ decode their own data stream and treat all other received signals, including the sensing stream, as interference. \emph{Targets} at $\mathbf q_n=(x_n,y_n,0)^T$ are passive scatterers with unknown horizontal position $(x_n,y_n)$; they neither transmit nor cooperate. The \emph{CC} (e.g., a ground station or a leader UAV) collects state information, runs the trajectory, power, and beamforming policy, and returns the resulting control to the UAVs.

\textbf{Assumptions.} The model relies on the following assumptions, each with its justification.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{(A1) Ideal backhaul and global CSI.} Backhaul links are error-free and delay-free, since this isolates the ISAC design from control-plane limitations and yields a performance upper bound.
  \item \textbf{(A2) Perfect CSI and position knowledge.} UAV and user positions, and the channels derived from them, are known exactly, because UAV positioning (GNSS/RTK) and pilot-based estimation are accurate relative to the geometry scales considered.
  \item \textbf{(A3) Block fading.} Channels are constant within a slot and independent across slots, so that one slot corresponds to one beamforming decision and one rate evaluation.
  \item \textbf{(A4) Expected-channel LoS/NLoS model.} The LoS probability enters through an averaged large-scale gain, because it keeps the channel a smooth function of the UAV position, which is required for gradient-based learning.
  \item \textbf{(A5) Spatial degrees of freedom.} $M+1\le N_t$, so that each UAV can transmit one stream per user plus one sensing stream without rank limitation.
  \item \textbf{(A6) Symbol statistics.} Data and probing symbols are zero-mean, unit-power, circularly symmetric, and mutually uncorrelated, because user data are independent and the probing waveform is a deterministic reference, which makes the transmit covariance a sum of beam outer products.
  \item \textbf{(A7) Separated sensing and communication beams.} The sensing stream is dedicated and the communication streams are not waveform-shared, since this decouples the sensing metric from the data content and exposes a clean power-split tradeoff.
  \item \textbf{(A8) Static targets with known altitude.} Targets do not move during the mission and lie on the ground plane, so only the horizontal position needs to be estimated.
  \item \textbf{(A9) Single-channel echo reception with perfect transmit--receive isolation.} Each UAV receives with one channel and suffers no self-interference, because this gives a conservative sensing SNR (no receive-array gain) while avoiding the modeling of full-duplex residual interference.
  \item \textbf{(A10) Coherent fusion across UAVs.} Echo observations of different UAVs are fused with shared nuisance parameters, which gives an optimistic but tractable CRB for cooperative localization.
  \item \textbf{(A11) Kinematics.} UAVs move with bounded speed $v_{\max}$, fixed altitude band, and a minimum separation $d_{\min}$; propulsion energy and array yaw are not modeled, because the focus is on the ISAC resource tradeoff rather than on flight energy.
\end{itemize}
