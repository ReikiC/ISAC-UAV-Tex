\subsection{System Description}

We consider the multi-UAV mmWave ISAC network illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:system}. The network consists of $K$ UAVs ($\mathcal K=\{1,2,\ldots,K\}$), $M$ ground users ($\mathcal M$), $N$ static ground targets ($\mathcal N$), and a central controller (CC) hosted by a leader UAV.

The UAVs are equipped with $N_{t}$-element uniform linear arrays (ULAs) and flying at a fixed altitude band. Each UAV transmits a superposition of $M$ communication streams and one dedicated sensing stream, and it receives the target echoes of that transmission. Every ground user and target is considered static and equipped with a single antenna. The CC collects state information, runs the trajectory, power, and beamforming policy, and returns the resulting control to the UAVs. The mission horizon is divided into $T$ slots of duration $\Delta t$.

\subsection{A2G mmWave Channel Model}

Let $\theta_{\rm el}$ denote the elevation angle (in degrees) of the UAV as seen from a ground node. The LoS probability follows the sigmoid model of Al-Hourani:
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm LoS}(\theta_{\rm el})=\frac{1}{1+a\exp\!\big(-b(\theta_{\rm el}-a)\big)},
\end{equation}
where $a$ and $b$ are environment-dependent constants.

Let $\mathbf p_k$ and $\mathbf u_m$ denote the positions of UAV $k$ and user $m$, respectively. The distance between them is $d_{k,m}=\max\{\|\mathbf p_k-\mathbf u_m\|,d_0\}$, where $d_0$ is the reference distance, and the reference channel gain is $\beta_0=(\lambda/4\pi d_0)^2$. The LoS/NLoS-weighted average path-loss gain is then given by
\begin{equation}
\bar\beta_{k,m}=P_{\rm LoS}\,\beta_0\Big(\frac{d_0}{d_{k,m}}\Big)^{\alpha_{\rm L}} +\big(1-P_{\rm LoS}\big)\beta_0\Big(\frac{d_0}{d_{k,m}}\Big)^{\alpha_{\rm N}},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\rm L}<\alpha_{\rm N}$ are the path-loss exponents of the LoS and NLoS links, respectively.

The channel from UAV $k$ to user $m$ follows a Rician model:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf h_{k,m}=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa\bar\beta_{k,m}}{\kappa+1}}\, \mathbf a(\theta_{k,m},\phi_{k,m}) +\sqrt{\frac{\bar\beta_{k,m}}{\kappa+1}}\,\mathbf g_{k,m},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is the Rician $K$-factor and $\mathbf g_{k,m}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\mathbf I_{N_t})$ is the NLoS scattering component. The ULA steering vector $\mathbf a(\theta,\phi)$ is given by
\begin{equation}
[\mathbf a(\theta,\phi)]_n=\exp\!\big(j\pi\, n\cos\phi\cos\theta\big), \quad n=0,\dots,N_t-1,
\end{equation}
where the antenna spacing is designed as half the carrier wavelength, i.e., $d_a=\lambda/2$.


\subsection{Transmit Signal and Downlink Communication}

UAV $k$ transmits the signal
\begin{equation}
\mathbf x_k=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbf w_{k,j}s_j+\mathbf w_{k,r}s_r,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf w_{k,j}\in\mathbb C^{N_t}$ is the communication beamformer for user $j$, $\mathbf w_{k,r}\in\mathbb C^{N_t}$ is the dedicated sensing beamformer, $s_j$ is the data symbol of user $j$, and $s_r$ is a known probing waveform. The symbols satisfy $\mathbb{E}[s_js_i^*]=\delta_{ji}$, $\mathbb{E}[s_js_r^*]=0$, and $\mathbb{E}[|s_r|^2]=1$. 

Under the above symbol statistics, the transmit covariance matrix of UAV $k$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf R_k=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf x_k\mathbf x_k^H]=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbf w_{k,j}\mathbf w_{k,j}^H+\mathbf w_{k,r}\mathbf w_{k,r}^H\succeq\mathbf 0,
\end{equation}
and the transmit power of each UAV is limited by
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf R_k)\le P_{\max},\quad \forall k\in\mathcal K.
\end{equation}

Let $k(m)$ denote the serving UAV of user $m$. The desired signal power of user $m$ is $S_m=|\mathbf h_{k(m),m}^H\mathbf w_{k(m),m}|^2$. The interference at user $m$ consists of three parts: (i) multiuser interference (MUI) from the other communication streams of the serving UAV, (ii) inter-UAV interference from all communication streams of the non-serving UAVs, and (iii) sensing-induced interference from the sensing beams of all UAVs, i.e.,
\begin{multline}
I_m=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\sum_{j\in\mathcal M}\big|\mathbf h_{k,m}^H\mathbf w_{k,j}\big|^2\,\mathbf 1\{(k,j)\neq(k(m),m)\}\\
+\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\big|\mathbf h_{k,m}^H\mathbf w_{k,r}\big|^2.
\end{multline}
The received SINR $\gamma_m$ and the achievable rate of user $R_m$ are then given by
\begin{equation}
\gamma_m=\frac{S_m}{\sigma^2+I_m},\qquad R_m=B\log_2(1+\gamma_m),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^2$ is the noise power and $B$ is the system bandwidth.

\subsection{Sensing Model}

The reflection coefficient of target $n$ with respect to UAV $k$ is
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{k,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^2\sigma_{\rm rcs}}{(4\pi)^3d_{k,n}^4}}\;e^{j\psi_{k,n}},
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\rm rcs}$ is the radar cross-section (RCS), $d_{k,n}=\|\mathbf q_n-\mathbf p_k\|$ is the distance between UAV $k$ and target $n$ located at $\mathbf q_n$, the factor $d_{k,n}^{-4}$ accounts for the two-way path loss, and $\psi_{k,n}$ is an unknown phase. After matched filtering with each known symbol and coherent integration over $N_s=BT_s$ samples, where $T_s$ is the integration time, the observation vector stacked over the $M{+}1$ streams is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y_{k,n}=\sqrt{N_s}\,\alpha_{k,n}\mathbf W_k^H\mathbf a(\theta_{k,n},\phi_{k,n})+\mathbf z,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf W_k=[\mathbf w_{k,1},\dots,\mathbf w_{k,M},\mathbf w_{k,r}]$ and $\mathbf z\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\sigma_r^2\mathbf I_{M+1})$. The echo SNR depends on the beamformers only through $\mathbf R_k$:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{k,n}=\frac{N_s|\alpha_{k,n}|^2\,\mathbf a_{k,n}^H\mathbf R_k\mathbf a_{k,n}}{\sigma_r^2},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf a_{k,n}\triangleq\mathbf a(\theta_{k,n},\phi_{k,n})$. This expression reveals the coupling between sensing and communication: the sensing performance is invariant to how the power is split among the streams and depends only on the aggregate spatial covariance, so the communication streams also contribute to sensing.

For each target, the unknown parameter vector is $\boldsymbol\xi_n=[x_n,\,y_n,\,|\alpha_n|,\,\arg\alpha_n]^T$, where the last two entries are nuisance parameters. The Fisher information matrix (FIM) consists of two parts.

\emph{Angle/amplitude-phase part.} Define
\begin{multline}
\mathbf H_{k,n}=\big[\alpha_{k,n}\partial_x\mathbf a_{k,n},\ \alpha_{k,n}\partial_y\mathbf a_{k,n},\\
|\alpha_{k,n}|\mathbf a_{k,n},\ j|\alpha_{k,n}|\mathbf a_{k,n}\big]\in\mathbb C^{N_t\times4}.
\end{multline}
The corresponding FIM is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J^{\rm ang}_n=\frac{2N_s}{\sigma_r^2}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\operatorname{Re}\big\{\mathbf H_{k,n}^H\mathbf R_k\mathbf H_{k,n}\big\}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Time-delay part.} The round-trip delay is $\tau_{k,n}=2d_{k,n}/c$. Assuming a flat spectrum over bandwidth $B$, the effective bandwidth satisfies $B_{\rm rms}^2=B^2/12$, and the delay-induced information on the horizontal position is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J^{\rm del}_n=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}8\pi^2B_{\rm rms}^2\Big(\frac{2}{c}\Big)^2\rho_{k,n}\,\mathbf g_{k,n}\mathbf g_{k,n}^T,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf g_{k,n}=(\mathbf q_n-\mathbf p_k)_{xy}/d_{k,n}$ is the gradient of $d_{k,n}$ with respect to the horizontal position of the target. The total FIM is therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J_n=\mathbf J^{\rm ang}_n+\operatorname{blkdiag}\big(\mathbf J^{\rm del}_n,\ \mathbf 0_{2\times2}\big).
\end{equation}

The nuisance parameters are eliminated by the Schur complement, since the inverse of the Schur complement equals the upper-left $2\times2$ block of $\mathbf J_n^{-1}$. Hence, the position Cram\'er--Rao bound (CRB) of target $n$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CRB}_n\big(\{\mathbf p_k,\mathbf R_k\}\big)=\big[\mathbf J_n^{-1}\big]_{1,1}+\big[\mathbf J_n^{-1}\big]_{2,2}\ \ [\mathrm{m}^2].
\end{equation}
Summing the angle term over $k$ before inversion implies that all UAVs share a single pair of nuisance parameters, i.e., coherent fusion. This is an optimistic approximation, since the true $\alpha_{k,n}$ differ across UAVs.

\subsection{UAV Mobility and Safety Constraints}
The trajectory of each UAV is subject to kinematic and safety constraints:
\begin{align}
&\|\mathbf p_k[t+1]-\mathbf p_k[t]\|\le v_{\max}\Delta t,\\
&z_{\min}\le z_k[t]\le z_{\max},\quad (x_k[t],y_k[t])\in\mathcal A,\\
&\|\mathbf p_i[t]-\mathbf p_j[t]\|\ge d_{\min},\quad\forall\,i\neq j,
\end{align}
where $\mathcal A$ is the horizontal mission area and $d_{\min}$ is the inter-UAV collision-avoidance distance.

\subsection{Assumptions}
\textbf{Assumptions.} The model relies on the following assumptions, each with its justification.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{(A1) Ideal backhaul and global CSI.} Backhaul links are error-free and delay-free, since this isolates the ISAC design from control-plane limitations and yields a performance upper bound.
  \item \textbf{(A2) Perfect CSI and position knowledge.} UAV and user positions, and the channels derived from them, are known exactly, because UAV positioning (GNSS/RTK) and pilot-based estimation are accurate relative to the geometry scales considered.
  \item \textbf{(A3) Block fading.} Channels are constant within a slot and independent across slots, so that one slot corresponds to one beamforming decision and one rate evaluation.
  \item \textbf{(A4) Expected-channel LoS/NLoS model.} The LoS probability enters through an averaged large-scale gain, because it keeps the channel a smooth function of the UAV position, which is required for gradient-based learning.
  \item \textbf{(A5) Spatial degrees of freedom.} $M+1\le N_t$, so that each UAV can transmit one stream per user plus one sensing stream without rank limitation.
  \item \textbf{(A6) Symbol statistics.} Data and probing symbols are zero-mean, unit-power, circularly symmetric, and mutually uncorrelated, because user data are independent and the probing waveform is a deterministic reference, which makes the transmit covariance a sum of beam outer products.
  \item \textbf{(A7) Separated sensing and communication beams.} The sensing stream is dedicated and the communication streams are not waveform-shared, since this decouples the sensing metric from the data content and exposes a clean power-split tradeoff.
  \item \textbf{(A8) Static targets with known altitude.} Targets do not move during the mission and lie on the ground plane, so only the horizontal position needs to be estimated.
  \item \textbf{(A9) Single-channel echo reception with perfect transmit--receive isolation.} Each UAV receives with one channel and suffers no self-interference, because this gives a conservative sensing SNR (no receive-array gain) while avoiding the modeling of full-duplex residual interference.
  \item \textbf{(A10) Coherent fusion across UAVs.} Echo observations of different UAVs are fused with shared nuisance parameters, which gives an optimistic but tractable CRB for cooperative localization.
  \item \textbf{(A11) Kinematics.} UAVs move with bounded speed $v_{\max}$, fixed altitude band, and a minimum separation $d_{\min}$; propulsion energy and array yaw are not modeled, because the focus is on the ISAC resource tradeoff rather than on flight energy.
\end{itemize}
