\subsection{System Description}

We consider the multi-UAV mmWave ISAC network illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:system}. The network consists of $K$ UAVs ($\mathcal K=\{1,2,\ldots,K\}$), $M$ ground users ($\mathcal M$), $N$ static ground targets ($\mathcal N$), and a central controller (CC) hosted by a leader UAV. 

Each UAV is equipped with an $N_t$-element uniform linear array (ULA) and flies within a designed altitude band. The number of antennas satisfies $N_t\ge M+1$. All ground users move according to the correlated random walk model in Section~\ref{sec:user_mobility} and each is equipped with a single antenna, whereas the ground targets are static passive reflectors that carry no transceiver. The backhaul between the UAVs and the CC is considered error-free and delay-free. The CC collects global state information, runs the trajectory, power, and beamforming policy, and returns the resulting control to the UAVs. 

We consider a mission horizon of $T\Delta t$ seconds, which is partitioned into $T$ non-overlapping slots of equal duration $\Delta t$. Slot $t\in\mathcal T\triangleq\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}$ spans the interval $[t\Delta t,(t+1)\Delta t)$. $\Delta t$ is sufficiently small; the geometry and channel within the timeslot are considered to remain constant, and are represented by the position at the start of the timeslot.Throughout the paper, the slot index $[t]$ is omitted whenever no ambiguity arises. In every slot, each user $m$ is associated with exactly one UAV, denoted by $k(m)\in\mathcal K$, so that the set of users served by UAV $k$ is $\mathcal M_k=\{m:k(m)=k\}$; the rule that fixes $k(m)$ is given in~\eqref{eq:assoc}. Each UAV transmits one communication stream to each user in $\mathcal M_k$ and one dedicated sensing stream, and it receives the target echoes of that transmission.



%  Accordingly, $\mathbf p_k[0]$ is given, $\mathbf p_k[T]$ is not defined, and the kinematic constraint \eqref{C1} applies for $t=0,\ldots,T-2$. Slot $0$ is a decision slot for the beamformers only; the first trajectory decision is $\mathbf p_k[1]$ at slot $1$. There are therefore $T$ beamformer decisions and $T-1$ trajectory decisions.

\subsection{Coordinate System and Kinematics}

\subsubsection{Coordinate System}
All positions are expressed in a common right-handed Cartesian coordinate system, in which the $x$--$y$ plane is horizontal and the $z$-axis points upward. The horizontal mission area is $\mathcal A=[0,L_x]\times[0,L_y]$. The ground users and targets lie on the ground plane, whereas the UAVs fly within the altitude band $[z_{\min},z_{\max}]$. The positions of UAV $k$, user $m$, and target $n$ are
\begin{align}
\mathbf p_k[t]&=\big(x_k[t],\,y_k[t],\,z_k[t]\big)^T,\\
\mathbf u_m[t]&=\big(x_m[t],\,y_m[t],\,0\big)^T,\\
\mathbf q_n&=\big(x_n,\,y_n,\,0\big)^T,
\end{align}
respectively, where $\mathbf p_k[t]$, $\mathbf u_m[t]$ denote the positions at time $t\Delta t$, i.e., at the beginning of slot $t$. The targets are static, and the users move according to the correlated random walk described in Section~\ref{sec:user_mobility}.

\subsubsection{Link Geometry}
For a link from point $\mathbf o_1$ to point $\mathbf o_2$, let $\mathbf r=\mathbf o_2-\mathbf o_1=(r_x,r_y,r_z)^T$. The link distance, azimuth angle, and elevation angle are
\begin{align}
d&=\|\mathbf r\|,\\
\theta&=\operatorname{atan2}(r_y,r_x)\in(-\pi,\pi],\\
\phi&=\operatorname{atan2}\!\Big(r_z,\sqrt{r_x^2+r_y^2}\Big)\in\Big[-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Big],
\end{align}
where $\theta$ is measured from the positive $x$-axis towards the positive $y$-axis, and $\phi$ is measured from the horizontal plane and is positive upward. When the link is exactly vertical ($r_x=r_y=0$), $\theta$ is undefined; this event has measure zero and is ignored. Accordingly, $d_{k,m}=\|\mathbf p_k-\mathbf u_m\|$ and $d_{k,n}=\|\mathbf p_k-\mathbf q_n\|$ denote the UAV--user and UAV--target distances, and $(\theta_{k,m},\phi_{k,m})$ and $(\theta_{k,n},\phi_{k,n})$ denote the corresponding departure angles measured at UAV $k$. Because the UAVs always fly above the ground nodes, $\phi_{k,m}<0$ and $\phi_{k,n}<0$, and $d_{k,m}\ge z_{\min}$. In contrast, the LoS probability in Section~\ref{sec:channel} depends on the elevation angle of the UAV seen from the ground node, denoted by $\theta_{\rm el}$ (in degrees). For the same link, the two elevation angles have opposite signs, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\rm el}=-\phi_{k,m}\cdot\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}\in(0^\circ,90^\circ].
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{UAV Kinematics}
The trajectory of each UAV is subject to kinematic and safety constraints:
\begin{align}
&\|\mathbf p_k[t+1]-\mathbf p_k[t]\|\le v_{\max}\Delta t,\quad t=0,\ldots,T-2,\\
&\mathbf p_k[t]\in\mathcal A\times[z_{\min},z_{\max}],\quad t=0,\ldots,T-1,\\
&\|\mathbf p_k[t]-\mathbf p_{k'}[t]\|\ge d_{\min},\quad\forall\,k\neq k',\ t=0,\ldots,T-1,
\end{align}
where $\mathcal A$ is the horizontal mission area and $d_{\min}$ is the inter-UAV collision-avoidance distance.

\subsubsection{Ground User Mobility}\label{sec:user_mobility}
The ground users follow a correlated random walk, which captures the directional persistence of pedestrian or vehicular motion. The horizontal velocity $\mathbf v^{\rm u}_m[t]\in\mathbb R^2$ of user $m$ evolves as a first-order Gauss--Markov process,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:user_vel}
\tilde{\mathbf v}^{\rm u}_m[t+1]=\zeta\,\mathbf v^{\rm u}_m[t]+\sqrt{1-\zeta^{2}}\,\sigma_{\rm u}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_m[t],\qquad
\boldsymbol\epsilon_m[t]\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\mathbf I_2),
\end{equation}
where $\zeta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{\rm v}}\in(0,1)$ is the memory coefficient determined by the velocity correlation time $\tau_{\rm v}$, and $\sigma_{\rm u}$ is the per-axis standard deviation of the stationary velocity. The innovations $\boldsymbol\epsilon_m[t]$ are independent across users and slots. In the limit $\tau_{\rm v}\to0$, $\zeta\to0$ and the velocity degenerates into an uncorrelated random walk, whereas $\tau_{\rm v}\to\infty$ yields $\zeta\to1$ and straight-line motion. The speed is capped at $v^{\rm u}_{\max}$ by rescaling,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:user_clip}
\mathbf v^{\rm u}_m[t+1]=\tilde{\mathbf v}^{\rm u}_m[t+1]\,\min\!\Big\{1,\ \frac{v^{\rm u}_{\max}}{\|\tilde{\mathbf v}^{\rm u}_m[t+1]\|}\Big\},
\end{equation}
and the two-dimensional horizontal position $\tilde{\mathbf u}_m[t+1]\in\mathbb R^2$ is updated as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:user_pos}
\tilde{\mathbf u}_m[t+1]=\big(x_m[t],\,y_m[t]\big)^T+\mathbf v^{\rm u}_m[t+1]\,\Delta t.
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf v^{\rm u}_m[t]$ is the velocity state at time $t\Delta t$. The updates \eqref{eq:user_vel}--\eqref{eq:user_pos} thus form a right-endpoint (implicit) discretization: $\mathbf v^{\rm u}_m[t+1]$, the velocity at time $(t+1)\Delta t$, is used to move from $\mathbf u_m[t]$ to $\mathbf u_m[t+1]$.

Let $\tilde x_m$ and $\tilde y_m$ denote the two components of $\tilde{\mathbf u}_m[t+1]$. To keep the users inside $\mathcal A$, a reflecting boundary is applied to each coordinate. For the $x$-coordinate, with range $[0,L_x]$,
\begin{equation}
x_m[t+1]=
\begin{cases}
-\tilde x_m, & \tilde x_m<0,\\
2L_x-\tilde x_m, & \tilde x_m>L_x,\\
\tilde x_m, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:user_reflect}
\end{equation}
and in the first two cases the $x$-component of $\mathbf v^{\rm u}_m[t+1]$ changes sign. The $y$-coordinate obeys the same rule with $x$, $\tilde x_m$, and $L_x$ replaced by $y$, $\tilde y_m$, and $L_y$. Since the per-slot displacement $v^{\rm u}_{\max}\Delta t\ll\min\{L_x,L_y\}$, a single reflection per coordinate suffices. The initial positions are drawn uniformly over $\mathcal A$, and the initial velocities are drawn from $\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\sigma_{\rm u}^2\mathbf I_2)$ and capped by the same rescaling as in \eqref{eq:user_clip}; with $v^{\rm u}_{\max}\gtrsim 4\sigma_{\rm u}$, the speed cap and the boundary reflections perturb this distribution only marginally, so that the velocity remains approximately stationary.

The users are assumed to be much slower than the UAVs, i.e., $v^{\rm u}_{\max}\ll v_{\max}$, so that the UAVs are able to track them. Unlike the UAV trajectories, the user trajectories are exogenous random processes and are not decision variables. The user positions $\{\mathbf u_m[t]\}$ are known to the CC at the beginning of slot $t$, whereas their future positions are unknown, and only the statistics in \eqref{eq:user_vel}--\eqref{eq:user_pos} are available.

\subsection{A2G mmWave Channel Model}\label{sec:channel}

The A2G channel is considered as a block-fading model. The deterministic Doppler of the LoS component is assumed to be compensated, whereas the scattering component is simply redrawn in every slot.

Let $\theta_{\rm el}$ denote the elevation angle (in degrees) of the UAV as seen from a ground node. The LoS probability follows the sigmoid model of Al-Hourani:
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm LoS}(\theta_{\rm el})=\frac{1}{1+a\exp\!\big(-b(\theta_{\rm el}-a)\big)},
\end{equation}
where $a$ and $b$ are environment-dependent constants.

Let the distance between UAV $k$ and user $m$ be defined as $d_{k,m}=\|\mathbf p_k-\mathbf u_m\|$. Since the free-space reference model is valid only beyond the reference distance $d_0$, the effective distance is floored at $\bar d_{k,m}=\max\{d_{k,m},d_0\}$, and the reference channel gain is $\beta_0=(\lambda/4\pi d_0)^2$. The LoS/NLoS-weighted average path-loss gain is then given by
\begin{equation}
\bar\beta_{k,m}=P_{\rm LoS}\,\beta_0\Big(\frac{d_0}{\bar d_{k,m}}\Big)^{\eta_{\rm L}} +\big(1-P_{\rm LoS}\big)\beta_0\Big(\frac{d_0}{\bar d_{k,m}}\Big)^{\eta_{\rm N}},
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\rm L}<\eta_{\rm N}$ are the path-loss exponents of the LoS and NLoS links, respectively.

The large-scale gains also fix the user association: in every slot, user $m$ is served by the UAV with the strongest average gain,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assoc}
k(m)[t]=\arg\max_{k\in\mathcal K}\ \bar\beta_{k,m}[t],\qquad\forall m,\ t,
\end{equation}
where ties are broken in favor of the smallest index. Since $\bar\beta_{k,m}[t]$ depends only on the slot positions $\mathbf p_k[t]$ and $\mathbf u_m[t]$, the association is a deterministic function of the slot geometry: it is fixed once the positions are known, requires no knowledge of the small-scale fading, and is not an optimization variable of the network.

The channel from UAV $k$ to user $m$ follows a Rician fading model:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf h_{k,m}=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa\bar\beta_{k,m}}{\kappa+1}}\, \mathbf a(\theta_{k,m},\phi_{k,m}) +\sqrt{\frac{\bar\beta_{k,m}}{\kappa+1}}\,\mathbf g^{\rm s}_{k,m},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is the Rician $K$-factor, and $\mathbf g^{\rm s}_{k,m}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\mathbf I_{N_t})$ represents the NLoS scattering component.

The ULA is mounted along the $x$-axis with an inter-element spacing of $d_a=\lambda/2$. The steering vector $\mathbf a(\theta,\phi)$ is therefore expressed as
\begin{equation}
[\mathbf a(\theta,\phi)]_i=\exp\!\big(j\pi\, i\cos\phi\cos\theta\big), \quad i=0,\dots,N_t-1.
\end{equation}
Here, $\theta_{k,m}$ and $\phi_{k,m}$ denote the azimuth and elevation angles of departure, respectively, measured at the UAV. The azimuth angle is measured from the positive $x$-axis, while the elevation angle is negative for downward departure directions. 

% Note that $\phi_{k,m}$ differs from the ground-side elevation angle $\theta_{\rm el}$ used in the LoS probability model. For the same link, these two angles satisfy $\phi_{k,m}=-\theta_{\rm el}$.

\subsection{Transmit Signal and Downlink Communication}

UAV $k$ transmits the signal
\begin{equation}
\mathbf x_k=\sum_{j\in\mathcal M_k}\mathbf w_{k,j}s_{k,j}+\mathbf w_{k,r}s_{k,r},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf w_{k,j}\in\mathbb C^{N_t}$ is the communication beamformer for its served user $j$, $\mathbf w_{k,r}\in\mathbb C^{N_t}$ is the dedicated sensing beamformer, $s_{k,j}$ is the data symbol of user $j$, and $s_{k,r}$ is a known probing waveform. Since the UAVs do not transmit jointly, a stream sent by a non-serving UAV to user $m$ would carry no useful signal and only cause interference; hence no such stream is transmitted. The symbols satisfy $\mathbb{E}[s_{k,j}s_{k,j'}^*]=\delta_{jj'}$ for $j,j'\in\mathcal M_k$, $\mathbb{E}[s_{k,j}s_{k,r}^*]=0$, and $\mathbb{E}[|s_{k,r}|^2]=1$. Moreover, streams from different UAVs are mutually uncorrelated (no joint transmission), i.e., $\mathbb{E}[s_{k,\ell}s_{k',\ell'}^*]=0$ for $k\neq k'$, $\ell\in\mathcal M_k\cup\{r\}$, and $\ell'\in\mathcal M_{k'}\cup\{r\}$.

Under the above symbol statistics, the transmit covariance matrix of UAV $k$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf R_k=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf x_k\mathbf x_k^H\right]
=\sum_{j\in\mathcal M_k}\mathbf w_{k,j}\mathbf w_{k,j}^H+\mathbf w_{k,r}\mathbf w_{k,r}^H\succeq\mathbf 0,
\end{equation}
and its total transmit power is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf R_k)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal M_k}\|\mathbf w_{k,j}\|^2+\|\mathbf w_{k,r}\|^2\le P_{\max},\quad \forall k\in\mathcal K.
\end{equation}

To characterize how each UAV allocates its power between the two functions, we define the \emph{sensing power fraction}
\begin{equation}
\varsigma_k\triangleq\frac{\|\mathbf w_{k,r}\|^2}{\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf R_k)}\in[0,1], \quad \forall k\in\mathcal K\ \text{with}\ \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf R_k)>0.
\end{equation}

Accordingly, the power budget of each UAV for the communication mission is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal M_k}\|\mathbf w_{k,j}\|^2=(1-\varsigma_k)\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf R_k),\quad \forall k\in\mathcal K,
\end{equation}
and that for the sensing mission by
\begin{equation}
\|\mathbf w_{k,r}\|^2=\varsigma_k\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf R_k),\quad \forall k\in\mathcal K.
\end{equation}

A larger $\varsigma_k$ therefore shifts power from communication to sensing: $\varsigma_k=0$ corresponds to pure communication, while $\varsigma_k=1$ corresponds to pure sensing via the dedicated sensing stream.

The desired signal power of user $m$ is $S_m=|\mathbf h_{k(m),m}^H\mathbf w_{k(m),m}|^2$. The interference at user $m$ consists of three parts: (i) multiuser interference (MUI) from the other communication streams of the serving UAV, (ii) inter-UAV interference from the communication streams that the non-serving UAVs send to their own users, and (iii) sensing-induced interference from the sensing beams of all UAVs, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interference}
\begin{aligned}
I_m={}&\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\ \sum_{j\in\mathcal M_k\setminus\{m\}}\big|\mathbf h_{k,m}^H\mathbf w_{k,j}\big|^2\\
&+\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\big|\mathbf h_{k,m}^H\mathbf w_{k,r}\big|^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the inner sum excludes $j=m$: since $m\in\mathcal M_{k(m)}$ while $m\notin\mathcal M_k$ for $k\neq k(m)$, this removes exactly the desired stream of user $m$, i.e., the pair $(k,j)=(k(m),m)$, from the interference sum.
The last term in \eqref{eq:interference} is the sensing leakage. With local CSI, UAV $k$ can null the $k=k(m)$ term for each of its served users by projecting its sensing beam onto the orthogonal complement of the served-user channel subspace, at the cost of a smaller sensing array gain; Sec.~\ref{sec:info} adopts this projection as a fixed beamforming rule.
The received SINR $\gamma_m$ and the achievable rate $R_m$ of user $m$ are then given by
\begin{equation}
\gamma_m=\frac{S_m}{\sigma^2+I_m},\qquad R_m=B\log_2(1+\gamma_m),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^2$ is the noise power and $B$ is the system bandwidth.

\subsection{Sensing Model}

\subsubsection{Echo and Observation Model}

The UAVs are interconnected by an ultra-fast backhaul link and share a common clock and local oscillator, so that their echo observations can be fused coherently. Each target is modeled as an isotropic point scatterer whose reflectivity does not depend on the observation direction. The targets are assumed to be resolvable from each other in delay or angle, so that their echoes do not overlap and each CRB can be treated separately; clutter is neglected, and the users do not generate echoes.

The reflection coefficient of target $n$ with respect to UAV $k$ is then factorized as
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{k,n}=\chi_n\,g_{k,n},\qquad
g_{k,n}=\frac{\lambda}{(4\pi)^{3/2}d_{k,n}^{2}}\,e^{-j4\pi d_{k,n}/\lambda},
\end{equation}
where $\chi_n=\sqrt{\sigma_{{\rm rcs},n}}\,e^{j\psi_n}$ is the unknown complex reflectivity of target $n$, with $\sigma_{{\rm rcs},n}$ the RCS and $\psi_n$ the reflection phase, and $d_{k,n}=\|\mathbf q_n-\mathbf p_k\|$ is the distance between UAV $k$ and target $n$ located at $\mathbf q_n$. The factor $g_{k,n}$ is known: its magnitude accounts for the two-way free-space path loss ($d_{k,n}^{-4}$ in power), and its phase is the round-trip propagation phase, which is deterministic given the geometry and is resolved thanks to the coherent link. Consequently, $|\alpha_{k,n}|^2=\lambda^2\sigma_{{\rm rcs},n}/((4\pi)^3d_{k,n}^4)$, and the only unknown quantity shared by all UAVs is the scalar $\chi_n$. As in the channel model, a distance floor at $d_0$ is applied here as well, which is inactive since $d_{k,n}\ge z_{\min}\ge d_0$, with $d_0$ on the meter scale.

Each UAV knows the symbols it transmits and uses them as matched-filter references. By the mutual uncorrelatedness of the transmit streams, these symbol sequences, including those transmitted by different UAVs, are approximately orthogonal over the $N_s=BT_s$ samples of coherent integration, where $T_s$ is the integration time, so that each stream is isolated by its matched filter. Only monostatic echoes are processed, i.e., UAV $k$ uses the echoes of its own transmission; bistatic echoes of the other UAVs' signals are discarded, which further renders the bound conservative. The observation vector stacked over the $|\mathcal M_k|+1$ streams of UAV $k$ is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
\mathbf y_{k,n}=\sqrt{N_s}\,\alpha_{k,n}\mathbf W_k^H\mathbf a(\theta_{k,n},\phi_{k,n})+\mathbf z,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf W_k=[\{\mathbf w_{k,j}\}_{j\in\mathcal M_k},\mathbf w_{k,r}]$ and $\mathbf z\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\sigma_r^2\mathbf I_{|\mathcal M_k|+1})$. The echo SNR depends on the beamformers only through $\mathbf R_k$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:echo_snr}
\rho_{k,n}=\frac{N_s|\alpha_{k,n}|^2\,\mathbf a_{k,n}^H\mathbf R_k\mathbf a_{k,n}}{\sigma_r^2},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf a_{k,n}\triangleq\mathbf a(\theta_{k,n},\phi_{k,n})$. This expression reveals the coupling between sensing and communication: the sensing performance is invariant to how the power is split among the streams and depends only on the aggregate spatial covariance, so the communication streams also contribute to sensing.

\subsubsection{Fisher Information Matrix}

For each target, the unknown parameter vector is $\boldsymbol\xi_n=[x_n,\,y_n,\,\ln|\chi_n|,\,\arg\chi_n]^T$, where the first two entries are the parameters of interest and the last two are nuisance parameters. Since all UAVs observe the same target, they share a single pair of nuisance parameters. The $\ln|\chi_n|$ parameterization is used only for notational convenience; any invertible reparameterization of the nuisance parameters leaves the position CRB unchanged.

To keep the model tractable and conservative, the dependence of $\alpha_{k,n}$ on the target position is ignored, i.e., $g_{k,n}$ is treated as a known constant when differentiating. In particular, the carrier-phase term $e^{-j4\pi d_{k,n}/\lambda}$ is not exploited for ranging; only the spatial beam pattern and the delay are used to localize the target.

Define $\mathbf H_{k,n}\in\mathbb C^{N_t\times4}$ as the Jacobian of the received signal manifold with respect to $\boldsymbol\xi_n$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian}
\mathbf H_{k,n}=\alpha_{k,n}\big[\partial_x\mathbf a_{k,n},\ \partial_y\mathbf a_{k,n},\ \mathbf a_{k,n},\ j\,\mathbf a_{k,n}\big].
\end{equation}
All four columns carry the common factor $\alpha_{k,n}$, so its phase cancels in $\mathbf H_{k,n}^H\mathbf R_k\mathbf H_{k,n}$ and only $|\alpha_{k,n}|^2$ remains. The angle-domain FIM is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fim_ang}
\mathbf J^{\rm ang}_n=\frac{2N_s}{\sigma_r^2}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\operatorname{Re}\big\{\mathbf H_{k,n}^H\mathbf R_k\mathbf H_{k,n}\big\}.
\end{equation}

The delay is absent from the narrowband model~\eqref{eq:obs}: it is obtained from the wideband probing waveform, with an estimate of variance $(8\pi^2B_{\rm rms}^2\rho_{k,n})^{-1}$, where $\rho_{k,n}$ is the echo SNR of~\eqref{eq:echo_snr} and $B_{\rm rms}^2=B^2/12$ for a flat spectrum over $B$. The resulting information on the horizontal position is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jdel}
\mathbf J^{\rm del}_n=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}8\pi^2B_{\rm rms}^2\Big(\frac{2}{c}\Big)^2\rho_{k,n}\,\mathbf e_{k,n}\mathbf e_{k,n}^T,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf e_{k,n}=(\mathbf q_n-\mathbf p_k)_{xy}/d_{k,n}$ is the gradient of $d_{k,n}$ with respect to the horizontal position of the target, with norm $\|\mathbf e_{k,n}\|=\cos\phi_{k,n}\le 1$. The total FIM is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jfim}
\mathbf J_n=\mathbf J^{\rm ang}_n+\operatorname{blkdiag}\big(\mathbf J^{\rm del}_n,\ \mathbf 0_{2\times2}\big).
\end{equation}

The two blocks in~\eqref{eq:jfim} are added as if the delay and the angle estimates of an echo were independent. Since only the group delay is retained above, the delay and angle derivatives are orthogonal over the band, and the residual coupling caused by the array aperture is negligible, as $(N_t-1)\lambda/2\ll c/(2B)$.

\subsubsection{Cram\'er--Rao Bound (CRB)}

Partition $\mathbf J_n$ into blocks according to the position and nuisance parameters:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J_n=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf J_{pp,n} & \mathbf J_{p\nu,n}\\ \mathbf J_{p\nu,n}^T & \mathbf J_{\nu\nu,n}\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf J_{pp,n}\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}$ is the position block and already contains the delay-induced term $\mathbf J^{\rm del}_n$, while $\mathbf J_{p\nu,n}$ and $\mathbf J_{\nu\nu,n}$ are associated with the nuisance parameters. Eliminating the nuisance parameters via the Schur complement, the position CRB of target $n$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CRB}_n=\operatorname{tr}\!\Big[\big(\mathbf J_{pp,n}-\mathbf J_{p\nu,n}\mathbf J_{\nu\nu,n}^{-1}\mathbf J_{p\nu,n}^T\big)^{-1}\Big]\quad[\mathrm{m}^2],
\end{equation}
Because the common phase of $\alpha_{k,n}$ cancels in every block, the CRB is independent of the reflection phase $\psi_n$ and of the propagation phases $4\pi d_{k,n}/\lambda$. Hence the coherent-fusion assumption enters only through the shared nuisance pair. Since the position dependence of $\alpha_{k,n}$ is discarded, the resulting bound is conservative with respect to the full signal model, which would additionally exploit the carrier phase and the amplitude variation.

For a single UAV, the position block becomes singular when the target lies in the plane $r_y=y_n-y_k=0$, i.e., the target shares the $y$-coordinate of the UAV: both the angular and the delay information then act along the $x$-direction, so the $y$-coordinate is unobservable and the ULA cannot distinguish the two sides of its axis. This is a physical limitation rather than a numerical one, and it persists for $K>1$ only if every UAV satisfies $y_n=y_k$.

Apart from this geometric degeneracy, $\mathbf J_n$ is also rank deficient when the target is illuminated by a single stream: a rank-one $\mathbf R_k$ contributes at most two real dimensions through~\eqref{eq:fim_ang} and one through~\eqref{eq:jdel}, so $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf J_n)\le3$ and the CRB is unbounded regardless of the geometry, while two streams at one UAV, or one stream at each of two UAVs, restore full rank.

In the sequel, the sensing metric uses the capped bound $\widetilde{\mathrm{CRB}}_n=\min\{\mathrm{CRB}_n,\Gamma_{\rm cap}\}$: targets with a singular or ill-conditioned $\mathbf J_n$, such as those in the two regimes above, are assigned the value $\Gamma_{\rm cap}$, which keeps the metric finite.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Information Structure}\label{sec:info}

Slot $t$ proceeds in the following order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The CC observes the user positions $\mathbf u_m[t]$ and decides the UAV positions $\mathbf p_k[t]$. The association $k(m)[t]$ is not a decision: it follows deterministically from the positions through the rule~\eqref{eq:assoc}. For $t=0$, $\mathbf p_k[0]$ is given, so no first-stage decision remains.
\item The fading $\mathbf g^{\rm s}_{k,m}[t]$ is realized, which yields the channels $\mathbf h[t]$.
\item The CC observes $\mathbf h[t]$ and decides the beamformers $\mathbf w_{k,j}[t]$ and $\mathbf w_{k,r}[t]$.
\item The rates $R_m[t]$ and the bounds $\mathrm{CRB}_n[t]$ of the slot are evaluated.
\end{enumerate}

Let the history available at the beginning of slot $t$ be
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_t=\Big\{\{\mathbf u_m[\tau]\}_{\tau\le t},\ \{\mathcal X^{\rm I}[\tau],\mathcal X^{\rm II}[\tau],\mathbf h[\tau]\}_{\tau<t}\Big\},
\end{equation}
i.e., the user positions up to and including slot $t$, and all decisions and channels of the previous slots. The decisions of slot $t$ fall into two groups,
\begin{align}
\mathcal X^{\rm I}[t] &= \big\{\mathbf p_k[t]\big\}, \\
\mathcal X^{\rm II}[t] &= \big\{\{\mathbf w_{k,j}[t]\}_{j\in\mathcal M_k[t]},\ \mathbf w_{k,r}[t]\big\},
\end{align}
where $\mathcal X^{\rm I}[t]$ is a function of $\mathcal H_t$, and $\mathcal X^{\rm II}[t]$ is a function of $(\mathcal H_t,\mathcal X^{\rm I}[t],\mathbf h[t])$. Note that $\mathcal M_k[t]$ is determined by the association $k(m)[t]$ of~\eqref{eq:assoc}, which is a deterministic function of the positions $\mathbf p_k[t]$ and $\mathbf u_m[t]$; it is therefore fixed once $\mathcal X^{\rm I}[t]$ is chosen and, in particular, is already defined when the beamformers are decided. Specifically, define two mappings
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}^{\rm I}_t : \mathcal H_t &\;\mapsto\; \mathcal X^{\rm I}[t], \\
    \mathcal{L}^{\rm II}_t : \big(\mathcal H_t,\,\mathcal X^{\rm I}[t],\,\mathbf h[t]\big) &\;\mapsto\; \mathcal X^{\rm II}[t],
\end{align}
which yield
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}^{\rm I}_t\!\big(\mathcal H_t\big) &= \big\{\mathbf p_k[t]\big\}, \\
    \mathcal{L}^{\rm II}_t\!\big(\mathcal H_t,\,\mathcal X^{\rm I}[t],\,\mathbf h[t]\big) &= \big\{\{\mathbf w_{k,j}[t]\}_{j\in\mathcal M_k[t]},\;\mathbf w_{k,r}[t]\big\}.
\end{align}
A \emph{policy} $\mathcal P$ is the collection of these two mappings over all slots, formalized as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:policy}
    \mathcal P = \big\{\,\mathcal{L}^{\rm I}_t,\;\mathcal{L}^{\rm II}_t\big\}_{t=0}^{T-1}.
\end{equation}
The beamformers determine the transmit covariance $\mathbf R_k[t]$ of Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\emph{Restricted policy class.} The class~\eqref{eq:policy} leaves the beamformers free to depend on the instantaneous CSI. For tractability we learn, instead, only the displacements together with the sensing power fractions $\varsigma_k[t]$, which are functions of $(\mathcal H_t,\mathcal X^{\rm I}[t])$ and are therefore decided before $\mathbf h[t]$ is realized; once $\mathbf h[t]$ is known, $\mathcal X^{\rm II}[t]$ follows fixed rules rather than an optimization: equal power split among the served users, regularized zero-forcing for the communication streams, and a sensing direction that either steers toward the targets or is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the served-user channels. With unit-norm directions $\hat{\mathbf d}_{k,j}[t]$ and $\hat{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]$, the beamformers are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restricted_beams}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf w_{k,j}[t]&=\sqrt{\tfrac{(1-\varsigma_k[t])P_{\max}}{|\mathcal M_k[t]|}}\,\hat{\mathbf d}_{k,j}[t],\quad j\in\mathcal M_k[t],\\
\mathbf w_{k,r}[t]&=\sqrt{\varsigma_k[t]P_{\max}}\,\hat{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\varsigma_k[t]=1$ if $\mathcal M_k[t]=\emptyset$. Hence $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf R_k[t])=P_{\max}$ always, i.e., \eqref{C4} holds with equality, and the learned policy belongs to a subclass $\mathfrak P_{\rm res}\subset\mathfrak P$.

The sensing direction in \eqref{eq:restricted_beams} is selected by a binary switch $\iota\in\{0,1\}$. Let $\bar{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]\neq\mathbf 0$ be a fixed superposition of the target steering vectors, and let $\mathbf Q_k[t]\in\mathbb C^{N_t\times S_k}$, with $S_k=|\mathcal M_k[t]|$, be an orthonormal basis of $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf h_{k,j}[t]\}_{j\in\mathcal M_k[t]}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nullspace_dir}
\hat{\mathbf d}^{(\iota)}_{k,r}[t]=
\frac{\big(\mathbf I-\iota\,\mathbf Q_k[t]\mathbf Q_k[t]^H\big)\bar{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]}
{\big\|\big(\mathbf I-\iota\,\mathbf Q_k[t]\mathbf Q_k[t]^H\big)\bar{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]\big\|},
\end{equation}
and $\hat{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]=\hat{\mathbf d}^{(\iota)}_{k,r}[t]$ in \eqref{eq:restricted_beams}. For $\iota=0$, \eqref{eq:nullspace_dir} reduces to pure steering, $\hat{\mathbf d}^{(0)}_{k,r}[t]=\bar{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]/\|\bar{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]\|$. For $\iota=1$, it removes from $\bar{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]$ its projection onto the served-user channel subspace. If the norm in the denominator of \eqref{eq:nullspace_dir} falls below $\epsilon\|\bar{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]\|$ for a small tolerance $\epsilon>0$, the projection is numerically rank-deficient and we fall back to the unprojected direction. Whenever this fallback is not triggered, the resulting beam satisfies $\mathbf h_{k,j}^H[t]\mathbf w_{k,r}[t]=0$ for every $j\in\mathcal M_k[t]$, so the $k=k(m)$ term of the sensing-induced interference in $I_m$ vanishes, at the cost of a smaller sensing array gain. The projection does not change $\varsigma_k[t]$ or the power budget, and it does not protect the users served by other UAVs. Since $\hat{\mathbf d}_{k,r}[t]$ is computed from $\mathbf h[t]$, the switch belongs to $\mathcal X^{\rm II}[t]$; $\iota$ is fixed per experiment and is not a learned action.


\subsection{Performance Metrics}

\textbf{Communication metric.} With user weights $\omega_m\ge 0$, the normalized weighted spectral efficiency of slot $t$ is
\begin{equation}
\bar R[t]=\frac{1}{B\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}\omega_m}\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}\omega_m R_m[t]\quad[\mathrm{bit/s/Hz}],
\end{equation}
where $R_m[t]$ is given in Section~\ref{sec:model}. Normalizing by $\sum_m\omega_m$ makes $\bar R[t]$ independent of $M$ and of the scale of $\omega$.

\textbf{Sensing metric.} The capped bound $\widetilde{\mathrm{CRB}}_n[t]=\min\{\mathrm{CRB}_n[t],\Gamma_{\rm cap}\}$ is normalized by $\Gamma_{\rm cap}$:
\begin{equation}
\bar C[t]=\frac{1}{N\,\Gamma_{\rm cap}}\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\widetilde{\mathrm{CRB}}_n\big(\{\mathbf p_k[t],\mathbf R_k[t]\}\big)\in(0,1].
\end{equation}
The targets are static and the bound depends on the beamformers only through $\mathbf R_k[t]$; since the beamformers use $\mathbf h[t]$, $\bar C[t]$ is random through the fading. The bound is evaluated slot by slot, so the information collected on the static targets is not accumulated across slots, which is conservative with respect to the accumulated bound of a tracking filter. The clipping, by contrast, is not conservative: whenever $\mathrm{CRB}_n[t]>\Gamma_{\rm cap}$ the metric reports $\Gamma_{\rm cap}$ instead of the true bound, so $\bar C[t]$ is optimistic in that regime.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

With the trade-off weight $\mu\ge 0$, the mission problem is
\begin{align}
\max_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathfrak P}\quad & \mathbb E_{\mathcal{P}}\Big[\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\Big(\bar R[t]-\mu\,\bar C[t]\Big)\Big] \label{opt:main}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad & \|\mathbf p_k[t+1]-\mathbf p_k[t]\|\le v_{\max}\Delta t, \ \forall k,\,t\le T-2, \tag{C1}\label{C1}\\
& \mathbf p_k[t]\in\mathcal A\times[z_{\min},z_{\max}], \ \forall k,\,t, \tag{C2}\label{C2}\\
& \|\mathbf p_k[t]-\mathbf p_{k'}[t]\|\ge d_{\min}, \ \forall k\neq k',\,t, \tag{C3}\label{C3}\\
& \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf R_k[t])\le P_{\max}, \ \forall k,\,t, \tag{C4}\label{C4}
\end{align}

where the expectation is over the user mobility and the small-scale fading, the constraints must hold almost surely, and $\mathbf p_k[0]$ is given.

The objective~\eqref{opt:main} is the time-averaged weighted spectral efficiency minus $\mu$ times the normalized localization bound: $\mu=0$ recovers pure communication, and a larger $\mu$ shifts resources to sensing. Because $\bar R$ is in bit/s/Hz and $\bar C\in(0,1]$, the value of $\mu$ has to be calibrated against the typical magnitude of $\bar R$. Constraints~\eqref{C1} and~\eqref{C2} describe the UAV kinematics and flight volume, and~\eqref{C3} is the collision-avoidance requirement. The power budget~\eqref{C4} limits the total power of each UAV, shared by the communication and sensing streams, and it must hold for every fading realization. The association is not part of the policy: it is fixed by the position-based rule~\eqref{eq:assoc}, under which user $m$ takes its desired signal from the single UAV $k(m)$, while the streams that the other UAVs send to their own users, together with all sensing streams, become interference. Section~\ref{sec:method} optimizes~\eqref{opt:main} over the restricted class $\mathfrak P_{\rm res}\subset\mathfrak P$ of Section~\ref{sec:info}, so its value lower-bounds the optimum over $\mathfrak P$.
